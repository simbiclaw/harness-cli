This spec deploys the four DeepSeekMath-V2 mechanisms as a deterministic QA pipeline. The determinism is not a property of the model — it's a property of the architecture: the model is confined to proposing findings, and every finding must survive a deterministic grounding pass against the confirmed knowledge base before it can affect a score. Nondeterminism is quarantined upstream of the verdict. rev. 5 conforms the whole spec to spine v6: this repository is the Argus consumer tier only — it reads an INTENTS/ tree, runs score then adjust, and emits a report; ingest and calibration are other tiers, not this repo's code.

The model proposes; deterministic grounding against the confirmed knowledge base disposes. No finding affects a score unless it anchors to a real transcript span and a real INTENTS node at the pinned git-SHA epoch. The score is a pure function of the surviving anchored findings and a versioned rubric — replayable bit-for-bit, never generated by the model. rev. 5 splits that pure step into v6's two stages — raw = score(facts, rubric) then adjusted = adjust(raw, history) — without moving the seam: both are proposers on the model side; the gate and the re-derivation stay deterministic.
What rev. 3 adds — six decisions, propagated through §3–§9. A single soft rules_criteria finding is model-judgment grounded against model-shaped text. Rev. 3 lets a finding carry corroborating anchors from other expertise types, but only on terms that buy real decorrelation rather than manufacture consensus.
  1. D1 · Multi-anchor findings. A Finding carries grounding_signals[] spanning expertise types, not one anchor (§3.2).
  2. D2 · Independence taxonomy. Each signal gets an independence_class set by error-source disjointness from the proposer: independent (acoustic DSP, weight 1.0) · correlated (model-matched exemplar/case, weight wc) · redundant (model-judged text, weight 0) — §4.1.
  3. D3 · S2 proposes signals, S3 only verifies them. The proposer populates grounding_signals[]; S3 deterministically checks each resolves and co-locates on the span, then applies a fixed aggregation. S3 never runs the exemplar/case match itself — that would put model judgment back in the gate (importlinter grounding ✗ matching_model).
  4. D4 · Aggregation ⟂ τ. Corroboration acts on the finding axis (clears a per-finding thin-grounding deferral); the §6 κ-gate acts on the criterion axis. Auto-final requires both clear. An independent anchor never substitutes for κ.
  5. D5 · Soft⊕soft = 0. Two model-judged text signals on one span are the §6.4 voting prohibition in costume; aggregated corroboration from redundant-only signals is exactly zero.
  6. D6 · Acoustic narrows the residue, not only the uncertainty. An independent acoustic anchor observes a facet of tone the text proxy can't, so multi-anchor shrinks the C3 tone residue too — asymptotically; warm-prosody sarcasm still escapes (§9).
What rev. 4 adds — six decisions, and one correction to rev. 1–3. This thread sharpened the seam between what the model may say and what may ship, and split the single word "residue" into a computable◆ part and an estimable part.
  1. D7 · The model MAY propose a score — it never ships (corrects I3). A proposed score is just another finding. Proposing it is fine and even useful; it is quarantined like every model output and never enters the verdict. Earlier revisions said "the model never emits a score"; the precise rule is "the model's score is never the one that ships."
  2. D8 · S3 grounds evidence, not numbers. A digit has no transcript span to anchor to. S3 grounds the violation + span + criterion the proposed score rests on; S4 re-derives the number from those. The model's number is superseded, not blessed — passing it through would rebuild the evaluator-skill failure mode (the model handing out the score).
  3. D9 · One computable rubric, three roles. S2 reads it as a checklist to hunt against, S3 as the referent to ground against, S4 as the weight table to compute with. Same artifact, three jobs — which is why the companion's human→machine compile is load-bearing for all three at once.
  4. D10 · Two gates, two axes (the residue split). Per-call coverage is computable (how much of the verdict rests on grounded findings vs ungrounded assertion) — this is the finding axis, already implemented as anchor-or-quarantine. Per-criterion residue is not computable per call but is estimable over a stream via escape rate — the criterion axis. Auto-final requires both clear (§5.1).
  5. D11 · Escape rate is the other tail of the agreement instrument. κ asks "when Argus and a human both look, do they agree?"; escape rate asks "when Argus auto-passes, how often did it miss something a human catches?" Same drift detector, two faces (§6).
  6. D12 · Residue is a floor, and model-wobble is not residue. The escape-rate estimate bounds only the failures a reviewer can discover — it is an upper bound on knowable residue, not the truth. And proposer self-disagreement measures how hard a call is, not how much the checklist misses (the C4 contested-vs-hard conflation); it is a triage signal only, never a residue gate (§9).
◆ "Computable" in this spec means three distinct layers — all Turing-computable, but playing different architectural roles. The word is used throughout this spec and the soft-criteria authoring companion, and it names three things the pipeline must keep apart:
1. Compute (模型) — propose findings with referents
The model reads transcript + INTENTS, performs a forward pass, and proposes findings each carrying claimed referents. This IS computation — a finite sequence of matrix operations — and it IS Turing-computable. But the model does NOT dispose: its output is quarantined (I1).
2. Verify (门) — confirm referents exist and match
The gate checks each proposed finding: does it anchor to a real transcript span AND a real INTENTS node at the pinned SHA, and does the cited evidence literally satisfy the required_evidence / fail_condition? This IS computation — a mechanical check. A digit has no span; a finding without a referent is ungrounded (I2).
3. Dispose (门) — decide the verdict
Pass, fail, or defer-to-human. This IS computation — a pure function (score(), adjust()) of the surviving anchored findings + versioned rubric + case precedents — replayable bit-for-bit. The model's proposed score never ships (D7, D8).
"Computable" in these two specs therefore means gate-checkable: a finding is computable when it carries a referent the gate can deterministically verify against. Uncomputable does NOT mean Turing-uncomputable — it means lacking a ground-truth referent. Coverage (the fraction of verdict resting on grounded findings) is computable per call; residue (what the checklist missed) is not computable per call but estimable over a stream via escape rate (D10).

§0 · FOUNDATIONWhy determinism is now possible

The blocking problem in every prior turn was the absence of a decorrelated external referent for soft judgment. The constraint just changed: the entire knowledge base Argus reads is confirmed before Argus ever reads it. Under spine v6 that confirmation is not a runtime boolean on a stored entry — it is write-time ownership: the INTENTS/ tree is produced by upstream tiers, owned per-file by its producer, and read by Argus at a pinned git-SHA epoch as an already-single, already-calibrated artifact (D15, ADR-0003). Three consequences follow, and the architecture is built on them.

  • Every INTENTS node is a grounding wire. Because it was confirmed at write time by a producer that owns it, it is external to and decorrelated from the proposer model's blind spot. Meta-verification against it is a real circuit breaker, not the model shaking its own hand.
  • The Error Case Library loop is an oracle, not a poison reservoir. The co-degradation risk from earlier turns required model-auto-detected errors re-entering training. The write-time ownership gate severs that loop: nothing enters the INTENTS/ tree except through a producer-owned commit that mints a new epoch — Argus, the consumer, has no write path back into its own referent (D13/D15).
  • The judgment layer shrinks but does not vanish. Tone grounds against the Acoustic indicator framework (now rubric, D16); appropriateness grounds against Rules&Criteria + Best-Practice exemplars. The irreducible residue — genuinely contested calls where confirmed experts disagree — is detected by Argus-vs-human disagreement and routed out, never auto-scored.

§0.5 · DESIGN RATIONALEWhy these mechanisms, and not the naive port

The DSMath-V2 mechanisms do not transfer to QA uniformly. A naive port — apply all four everywhere — manufactures false rigor on exactly the criteria where the model is most prone to correlated error. The governing question is whether meta-verification can ground a critique against something outside the model's own blind spot. That single test sorts every criterion into two layers and decides each mechanism's fate.

Mechanism◢ Compliance layer
(transcript-checkable vs SOP)		◢ Judgment layer
(tone · empathy · appropriateness)
① Process

② Meta-

③ Self-
Verification		 WORKS — "did I include the disclaimer" is a string match dressed as judgment; safe to gate on. THEATER — prompted self-check on tone is the failure mode the paper names; faithfulness was an RL artifact, not a prompt.
④ Auto-

Read each row twice — down each column. The same mechanism earns opposite verdicts depending on whether a referent exists. The right-column failures are not weak-but-harmless; they are confidently wrong, which is worse than absent.

The four corrections this spec encodes

①
SOP is not an axiom. In math, following every rule guarantees correctness. In service, you can pass every extractable SOP check and still deliver a catastrophic interaction. So process-derivation buys scoring accuracy and coaching value — the cited sentence is useful to a human reviewer even when the score is contestable. → encoded as the Finding triple (§3.2) and the auditable score_derivation trail (§3.5).
②
Meta-verification needs a grounding wire. It is a circuit breaker only against an external referent; on tone it is the model shaking its own hand. The human-confirmed expertise types are those wires — that is the whole unlock. → encoded as S3's per-type applicability gradient (§4), deterministic where a wire exists, deferred where it doesn't.
③
Prompted self-verification is theater. DSMath-V2's generator self-evaluates faithfully because RL rewarded honest error-acknowledgment — not because it was asked nicely. We do not have that training, so "self-verification" is reinterpreted as the structural grounding pass (S3), and any copilot self-check is restricted to enumerable compliance items. → encoded in the S4 footnote and the §6 prohibition on trusting model self-report for scoring.
④
Auto-labeling without a validation gate poisons the set. The paper automated labeling only after confirming alignment with human experts. The judgment layer has no model-internal oracle, so the gate is human confirmation (S6) plus Argus-vs-human agreement (§6) — never Argus-vs-Argus voting. → encoded as the S1 reject-fixture, the S6 human-confirmation gate, and the §6 agreement instrument.
The operationalization residue — the honest caveat on §4. Decomposing "appropriateness" into fifteen binary checks asserts that the conjunction of the fifteen equals appropriateness. It rarely does. Operationalization doesn't eliminate judgment; it relocates it into the checklist design, where at least it is auditable and held in one place. The determinism gradient in §4 is therefore as good as the INTENTS nodes are complete — and the ungrounded bucket (I2) plus the human queue (§9) exist precisely to catch what the checklist failed to encode. The pipeline's honesty is not that it captures everything; it is that it knows, and reports, what it didn't.

§1 · DETERMINISM CONTRACTThe five invariants Claude Code must enforce

These are the load-bearing invariants. Every component is built to preserve them, and §7 gives each one a standing fixture. State them in CLAUDE.md as non-negotiable.

I1
QUARANTINE. Model nondeterminism exists only in Stage 2 (finding proposal). Stages 3–5 are pure functions of their inputs. A module in scoring/ or grounding/ that imports the model client is an architecture violation.
I2
ANCHOR-OR-QUARANTINE. Every finding references a real transcript span (exact-quote verified) and a real INTENTS node at the pinned SHA epoch, or it is moved to the ungrounded bucket and routed to a human. Findings are never silently dropped.
I3
SHIPPED SCORE = pure re-derivation, in two stages (D17). raw = score(grounded findings, rubric), then adjusted = adjust(raw, anchored precedents). Both are pure functions of their inputs; score never receives history. The model may propose a score (a quarantined finding, D7), but a proposed score enters neither stage and is never the shipped number. Same grounded findings + same rubric version + same anchored precedents ⇒ identical raw and adjusted.
I7
GROUND EVIDENCE, NOT NUMBERS. S3 grounds the violation, span, and criterion a finding rests on — never a bare score (a digit has no span). A model-proposed score is grounded only by grounding its evidence; the pure stages re-derive the number. No code path passes a model-proposed score through S3 into the verdict (D8). Proposed-vs-derived divergence is logged as a drift probe, never as a result.
I4
PINNED REFERENTS (v6: git-SHA epoch). Each evaluation pins the INTENTS/ tree at a single git-SHA epoch (EPOCH.yaml) covering every node read. Re-running against the same epoch reproduces the same grounding outcomes. This replaces rev. 4's content-addressed expertise-snapshot hash (D18).
I5
REPLAYABILITY. The stored FindingGraph + intents_sha + rubric version re-derive the identical EvaluationResult forever. This is the liveness canary for the whole pipeline.
I6
INDEPENDENCE-WEIGHTED CORROBORATION. Multi-anchor confidence is a pure, independence-weighted function of the signals — redundant (same model-judged text) signals contribute zero, so no number of correlated re-reads manufactures confidence. Corroboration is orthogonal to the §6 agreement gate: it may clear a per-finding thin-grounding deferral but never a criterion's κ < τ deferral. aggregate ✗ model_client.
The importlinter contract makes I1 mechanical — now on v6's five layers. Spine v6 enforces types → config → io → core → cli, and rev. 5 maps this pipeline onto them (D18): the INTENTS Provider is io; the LLM proposer is also a Provider (io), never core; the pure score, adjust, corroboration aggregator, and report emission are core; schemas are types. The decorrelation between "what proposes" and "what disposes" is enforced in the dependency graph, not in a code review.

The load-bearing fence (R7). v6 calls score/adjust "pure, in core." That claim is only true because the model call lives in a Provider: core ✗ model_client. So rev. 4's discipline is exactly what protects v6's purity — it does not contradict it. The fences, restated in v6 layer names: core ✗ model_client (was scoring ✗ model_client) · core ✗ proposer (grounding and scoring never import the proposer) · grounding ✗ matching_model (the exemplar/case match producing a correlated signal happens in the proposer, never in the gate — D3) · aggregate ✗ model_client (the corroboration aggregator is pure — I6).

§2.5 · THE SEAM UNDER v6Two score functions, one principle

v6 renamed the machinery; it did not move the seam. score() and adjust() are both proposers on the model side; the deterministic gate still disposes, and a pure function still re-derives the shipped number. v6 does not replace the governing principle — it makes the proposals falsifiable enough that the gate can finally reach the judgment layer.

Both v6 functions thread through the same three layers. Nothing on the model's side of the seam sets a shipped number. score emits per-rule verdicts; adjust proposes precedents; the gate anchors both; the pure re-derivation alone produces raw and adjusted.

2.5.1 — The three layers, and where each v6 function sits

  • Propose (model · io). score(facts, rubric) emits per-rule verdicts — "turn N contains evidence of type X: pass/fail" — each carrying an evidence anchor. adjust(raw, history) proposes which case precedents apply. Both are model output; neither has scored anything. Because each rule carries required_evidence and fail_condition, the proposals are falsifiable and low-variance — but low-variance or not, they remain proposals.
  • Dispose (deterministic gate · core). Each proposed verdict must anchor to a real transcript span and a real INTENTS rule at the pinned SHA, and the cited evidence must literally satisfy that rule's required_evidence / fail_condition. On the raw lane the gate also enforces the purity rule: any verdict citing a precedent is rejected. Each adjust precedent must anchor to a real L3 case-history entry, recorded in applied_precedents[]. Nothing survives on the model's say-so.
  • Re-derive (pure fn · core). raw is a pure function of the surviving anchored verdicts plus versioned rubric weights — replayable bit-for-bit. adjusted is a pure function of raw plus surviving anchored precedents. The model emits neither number. This is the principle, unchanged from rev. 4.

2.5.2 — Why this is complementary, not redundant

The tempting objection is that if v6's criteria are unambiguous, the re-derivation gate is dead weight. It isn't — and this is the whole point. In rev. 4 the hazard on the judgment layer was that the gate had nothing objective to check against, so meta-verification degenerated into an echo chamber. A rule carrying required_evidence and fail_condition gives the gate a literal, transcript-checkable thing to verify — "is there an acknowledgment span before the resolution span?" — instead of asking the model "was this empathetic?" So v6's falsifiable criteria are precisely what let the deterministic gate reach the judgment layer at all. The criteria make the proposal checkable; the gate does the checking. Complementary by construction.

2.5.3 — The purity invariant is enforced twice, on purpose

v6 enforces history-purity at authoring time through the signature: history is not an argument to score(). Under the combined model the gate enforces it again at runtime: any raw-lane verdict citing a precedent is rejected and quarantined. The signature stops it going in; the gate catches it if it somehow appears — belt and suspenders, and it keeps the two-stage split honest against a future refactor that might quietly widen the score() signature.

Carryover from rev. 4. The quarantined proposed_score (D7) still fits cleanly: the model may emit an aggregate as a finding for triage, but it is quarantined by definition — the shipped raw and adjusted are always re-derived. True in both the pre-v6 and v6 worlds; no change needed.

§4.1 · CORROBORATIONWhich anchors actually decorrelate (D2)

A second anchor on a finding is worth exactly its error-source disjointness from the proposer — not its determinism column, and not its standalone confidence. The knowledge modules sort into three independence classes. The class is a property of the act that produces the signal, not of the referent it cites: a confirmed referent reached by a model judgment is still correlated with the model's blind spot.

D16 — the acoustic split, and why it does not break corroboration. v6 reclassifies Acoustic Feature and Phrase/Keyword from facts to rubric. The distinction is measurement-vs-yardstick: the per-call acoustic measurement (a prosody number on the waveform) stays an independent fact in the call record and remains a full-weight corroborating signal below. But the 12-indicator acoustic framework and the phrase lexicon are the yardstick — versioned rubric in _rubric/ that score() reads to interpret the measurement. The framework is therefore not a corroborating anchor; counting it as one would double-count the rubric against itself. Corroboration weights below apply to the measurement, never the framework.

The aggregator is a pure function over the verified signals (S3 has already confirmed each resolves and co-locates on the span):

def aggregate_corroboration(signals: list[Signal], primary_channel: str) -> Corroboration:
    # exclude the channel the proposer already judged on — it cannot corroborate itself
    others = [s for s in signals if s.channel != primary_channel]
    w = {"independent": 1.0, "correlated": W_C, "redundant": 0.0}
    agg = 1.0 - math.prod(1.0 - w[s.independence_class] for s in others)  # noisy-OR; redundant-only ⇒ 0.0
    return Corroboration(aggregate=agg,
                         independent_present=any(s.independence_class == "independent" for s in others),
                         contributing=[s.signal_id for s in others if w[s.independence_class] > 0])
    # pure: no clock, no RNG, no model. W_C is a pinned rubric constant, versioned with the rubric.
D4 — aggregation is orthogonal to τ, and the orthogonality is load-bearing. There are two reasons a soft finding defers: the criterion isn't trusted (κ < τ, §6) or this finding is thinly grounded on a trusted criterion (finding_thin). Corroboration clears the second — an independent anchor gives the finding support the single channel lacked. It must never clear the first: an acoustic anchor on a criterion whose Argus-vs-human agreement is below τ does not earn that criterion the right to auto-finalize. Collapsing the two axes turns corroboration into a laundering path around the agreement instrument. Auto-final requires both axes clear.
WHERE THIS STILL LEAKS — read before trusting the aggregate

Convergent bias survives independence. Acoustic and text are different instruments, but if both were calibrated on the same population's norms, a finding both confirm can still be wrong in the same direction. Independence of channel is not independence of prior. The aggregate measures the former; only the §6.3 drift detector, watching Argus-vs-human over time, catches the latter.

wc is a guess until measured. The 0.4 for correlated signals is a starting constant, not a derived one. It should be set empirically as 1 − corr(matcher_error, proposer_error) on a human-labeled sample, then versioned with the rubric. Shipping it as a magic number is the rev. 3 analogue of the very thing this pipeline was built to stop asserting.

Corroboration narrows the tone residue but cannot close it (D6). Flat prosody catches the cold acknowledge-before-resolve that text misses. Warm-prosody sarcasm passes both. The residue closes toward zero, never to it — so an independently corroborated soft finding is still a judgment-layer finding, still κ-gated, never promoted to compliance-grade 1.0.

§5 · SCORE DERIVATIONTwo pure stages — score then adjust (I3, D17)

The pure re-derivation is v6's two-stage contract. Stage 1 score takes only the confirmed findings and the rubric — history is not an argument. Stage 2 adjust takes raw and the anchored L3 case precedents. Both are in core; neither contains a model call or any I/O beyond the pinned rubric and the anchored precedent set. This is the heart of determinism, and each stage carries its own importlinter fence (core ✗ model_client).

# STAGE 1 — raw = score(facts, rubric). HISTORY IS NOT AN ARGUMENT (D17 purity).
def score(graph: FindingGraph, rubric: Rubric, health: dict[str, CriterionHealth]) -> RawResult:
    confirmed = [f for f in graph.findings if f.meta_verdict == "confirmed"]
    assert all(not f.cites_precedent for f in confirmed)   # runtime purity gate: a raw-lane verdict citing a precedent is a violation
    deductions = [(f.finding_id, rubric.weight(f.anchor_node)) for f in confirmed]
    raw = rubric.apply(deductions)          # monotone, pure, total — reads NO proposed_score (I3/D7)

    coverage = grounded_mass(confirmed) / (grounded_mass(confirmed) + ungrounded_mass(graph))
    untrusted = {c for c in cited_criteria(confirmed) if health[c].status != "trusted"}
    probe = [(f.finding_id, f.proposed_score, rubric.weight(f.anchor_node)) for f in confirmed if f.proposed_score is not None]
    return RawResult(raw=raw, score_derivation=deductions, coverage=coverage,
                     untrusted=untrusted, proposed_vs_derived=probe)

# STAGE 2 — adjusted = adjust(raw, history). Precedents must anchor to real L3 case-history entries.
def adjust(raw: RawResult, precedents: list[Precedent], graph: FindingGraph, rubric: Rubric) -> EvaluationResult:
    anchored = [p for p in precedents if resolves_in_history(p, graph.intents_sha)]   # gate: unanchored precedent → dropped (I2)
    adjusted = rubric.apply_precedents(raw.raw, anchored)      # pure; empty precedents ⇒ adjusted == raw

    routing = "auto_final" if (
        not graph.ungrounded and not any_deferred(graph)   # coverage gate clear
        and not raw.untrusted                              # every cited criterion trusted
    ) else "human_review"
    return EvaluationResult(raw=raw.raw, adjusted=adjusted,
                            applied_precedents=[p.as_record() for p in anchored],
                            score_derivation=raw.score_derivation, routing=routing,
                            coverage=raw.coverage, proposed_vs_derived=raw.proposed_vs_derived,
                            replay_hash=hash_inputs(graph, rubric, anchored))   # excludes proposed_score

Properties Claude Code must hold on both stages: total (empty findings → raw 1.0; empty precedents → adjusted == raw), monotone (adding a violation never raises raw), pure (no clock, no RNG, no network, no model), history-pure (score never receives or reads history — enforced by signature and the runtime assert), replayable (replay_hash is a function of grounded inputs + anchored precedents only — never of proposed_score).

Corroboration changes routing, never the deduction. The deduction weight for a confirmed finding is the rubric's weight for its anchor — corroboration does not scale it (a violation is a violation regardless of how many instruments saw it). What corroboration does is upstream of S4: in S3/S5 it can flip a defer_reason: "finding_thin" to confirmed when an independent signal is present, moving the finding from the human queue into the scored set. It cannot flip criterion_below_tau (D4) and cannot rescue ungrounded (I2). So the only thing the aggregate touches is which bucket a finding lands in, never the arithmetic once it is scored.

§5.1 · COVERAGE vs RESIDUEThe two gates auto-final must pass (D10)

The word "residue" hides two different quantities. Telling them apart is what makes auto-final safe instead of a guess. The tempting design — "compute this call's residue, route it to a human if the residue is above a threshold" — is not buildable, and the reason it isn't is the whole lesson.

Residue is, by definition, the part the checks don't see. So when a call passes every check cleanly, the checks emit no signal about what they missed. A genuinely warm interaction and a coldly-sarcastic-but-correctly-sequenced one are identical to the machine — both pass acknowledge-before-resolve, both pass the keyword checks. No function of the checks can separate them; to compute a per-call residue number you would already need the judgment you removed. A per-call residue gate therefore smuggles the human back in.

What is computable per call is a different quantity that is easy to confuse with residue:

GateAxisWhat it measuresComputable?Already in the pipeline as

Escape ratecriterion (over a stream)how often an auto-passed call hid a real failure a human later caughtESTIMATE — lagging, statisticalthe §6 instrument, measured on the auto-pass tail; CriterionHealth.escape_rate

Coverage is the computable shadow of residue. If empathy here is fully decided by the acknowledge-before-resolve trigger plus acoustic corroboration, the checkable surface covered the call → auto-final eligible. If the verdict leans on a model assertion with nothing anchorable, that call has high uncovered surface → route to human. You already built this gate; rev. 4 only names it and surfaces the number.

Escape rate is where your residue-threshold instinct genuinely lives — but at the criterion level, lagging, and statistical, not per call. You cannot see the residue from inside the checklist; you can see its consequences. Sample what a criterion auto-passed, let humans review, count the real failures the criterion missed. A low, falling escape rate ⇒ small residue ⇒ its auto-finals are trustworthy. A rising escape rate ⇒ the residue is growing ⇒ demote the criterion, route its calls to humans, re-author. That rate is a thresholdable residue estimate — and it is the same instrument as the agreement gate, read on the other tail (§6).

Auto-final = coverage gate AND health gate. A call auto-finalizes only when it is fully covered (finding axis: no ungrounded, no deferred) and every criterion it cites is trusted (criterion axis: κ ≥ τ and escape rate acceptable). This is the rev. 3 D4 orthogonality, now with both axes named: coverage governs this call, escape rate governs this criterion's right to auto-pass at all. Neither substitutes for the other.
TWO HONEST LIMITS — read before trusting the escape rate

The escape rate is a floor, not the truth (D12). It only catches residue a reviewer eventually flags. A failure invisible to the checklist and to every reviewer is invisible to the estimator too — so the measured residue bounds the knowable residue from above and no further. Report it as "no residue we can currently find," never as "no residue."

Model self-disagreement is not a residue meter (D12 / C4). Sampling the proposer a few times and watching the verdict wobble feels like a residue signal; it isn't. It measures how hard the call is for the model, not how much the checklist misses the concept. A call can be easy for the model and high-residue (confident sarcasm) or hard for the model and zero-residue (a messy but fully checkable transcript). Treating wobble as residue is the contested-vs-hard conflation. Log it for triage priority; never wire it to auto-final.

§6 · AGREEMENT INSTRUMENTWhich criteria may auto-finalize

The instrument that gates auto-labeling already exists in the expertise design: Rules&Criteria is applied by both human inspectors and Argus. Measure their per-criterion agreement continuously; it decides whether a criterion is groundable enough to auto-finalize or must defer to a human.

  1. Compute per-criterion Cohen's κ (or raw agreement) between Argus verdicts and human-inspector verdicts on a rolling sample, stored as criterion_agreement[criterion_id] → float, versioned with the rubric.
  2. Gate. A soft finding's S3 confidence = the criterion's agreement score. Below threshold τ (start τ = 0.8) ⇒ deferred ⇒ human_review. Compliance criteria are exempt (already deterministic, κ ≈ 1).
  3. Drift detector. A criterion whose agreement falls across windows means Argus is drifting from the confirmed norm; it auto-demotes to human_review and flags for re-grounding. This is the standing liveness check for the judgment layer.
  4. No model-vote consensus anywhere. Agreement is Argus-vs-human, never Argus-vs-Argus. N model samples voting is explicitly forbidden as a labeling mechanism — it manufactures correlated consensus. (Carry this as a one-line prohibition in CLAUDE.md.) Rev. 3 corollary: soft⊕soft corroboration (D5) is the same prohibition wearing a corroboration costume — a soft finding backed by another model-judged text signal on the same span aggregates to exactly 0, never to a sum.
  5. Aggregation is orthogonal to τ (D4). The κ-gate decides whether a criterion may auto-finalize; corroboration decides whether a finding on an already-trusted criterion has cleared thin grounding. An independent anchor on a κ < τ criterion stays deferred. The two thresholds never substitute for one another, and no code path lets a corroboration score raise a criterion's effective κ.
  6. The instrument has two tails (D11). κ is the agreement tail — measured when Argus and a human both score the same sampled calls. Escape rate is the auto-pass tail — measured by sampling calls Argus auto-passed, having a human review them, and counting the real failures the criterion missed (escape_rate = human-caught misses ÷ sampled auto-passes). Both write to CriterionHealth; both feed the §6.3 drift detector. A criterion is trusted only while both hold: κ ≥ τ and escape rate below its ceiling. A rising escape rate demotes the criterion even if κ still looks healthy — auto-passes are exactly the calls κ never samples, so escape rate is the only instrument watching them.
Why escape rate is not redundant with κ. κ samples calls a human also scores — it can only measure criteria on the calls humans look at. Auto-passed calls are, by construction, the ones humans didn't score, so κ is blind to them. Escape rate is the deliberate, sampled human look at that blind region. Drop it and a criterion can hold a high κ on reviewed calls while quietly rotting on the auto-passed majority. The two tails together close the loop; either alone leaves a blind side.

§9 · RESIDUEWhat stays human, by design

The pipeline is honest about its own edge. Three things route to humans permanently — not as scaffolding to be optimized away, but as the structural reporting of where ground truth stops:

  • Ungrounded findings (I2). The proposer saw something it couldn't anchor. Could be a real issue no INTENTS node covers yet — the seed of a new Error Case Library entry, minted upstream at write time (D15).
  • Deferred soft findings (§4/§6). The contested regime: confirmed experts don't agree strongly enough for Argus to auto-finalize. Guessing here is the failure mode the whole thread was built to avoid.
  • Drift demotions (§6). A criterion where Argus has diverged from the confirmed norm. Routes to human until re-grounded.

Rev. 3 changes the size of the deferred bucket, not its existence. Independent corroboration (§4.1) rescues findings that were deferred only for thin grounding on a trusted criterion — and, for tone specifically, an acoustic anchor narrows the operationalization residue itself by observing a facet the text proxy can't (D6). But the narrowing is asymptotic: warm-prosody sarcasm passes both the text signature and the acoustic threshold, so a corroborated soft finding is still a judgment-layer finding, still κ-gated, never promoted to compliance-grade 1.0. The honest claim is "fewer false defers," not "the residue is gone."

Rev. 4 makes the residue measurable but never closed. The escape rate (§6) turns "how complete is this criterion" from an assertion into a tracked number — but a number that is a floor: it counts only the misses a reviewer catches, so it bounds the residue we can find, not the residue that exists (D12). Two failure modes stay outside it permanently: a failure invisible to both checklist and reviewer (the true unknown-unknown), and a contested call where no fact of the matter exists to be caught (C4). The pipeline's honesty in rev. 4 is that it reports the floor as a floor — a falling escape rate earns more auto-final, never a claim that the judgment is now complete. And the one signal that looks like it would close the gap — proposer self-disagreement — is explicitly walled off from routing, because it measures difficulty, not residue (the C4 conflation), and wiring it in would let the model's confidence, not the human's, decide what ships.

The closing discipline. A pipeline that never escalates is not more automated — it is less honest about where its judgments stop being checkable. DeepSeekMath-V2 reached gold-medal performance and still routed ambiguous proofs to human experts. This pipeline does the same: the human queue is not a weakness, it is the pipeline correctly reporting the boundary of its ground truth — and the size of that queue, tracked over time, is the truest measure of how much of the judgment layer the INTENTS/ tree has actually grounded.
